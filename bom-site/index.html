The design I would build

Four independently driven 80 mm mecanum wheels on the four owned NEMA 17 steppers, a low 350 × 390 mm 2020 frame, the battery slung under the deck for a low centre of gravity, the Ceres pan/tilt turret at the front carrying your webcam and a thermal array so it can look around and track, and both radars on a 200 mm post at the rear looking forward over the turret. Two short-range ToF sensors guard front and rear. The fifth motor and TB6600 stay spare.

No kit does this. Every mecanum chassis kit on Amazon is built for TT or DC gearmotors; none has a NEMA 17 mount. Steel L-brackets under the rails are the cleanest way to put your steppers on mecanum wheels, and the 5 mm couplers in the wheel set match the 5 mm flatted shaft on your motors.

Power is one LiPo for the motors and servos — your gel-blaster pack to start, through one of your LM2596 bucks for the 6 V servo rail — and a small USB power bank for the Pi, which keeps the computer isolated from motor-bus noise. The bank's mass is the price: with a compact 5,000 mAh unit the drivers must run at 1.7 A with the fan to keep the ramp margin. A 300 g brick does not fit.

Finding people is a four-sensor job, and that is how professional search-and-rescue kit is built: CO2 to narrow the area, thermal to confirm a body, a microphone for voice and knocking, radar for behind obstacles. The rover had only the radar leg. So the pan/tilt turret now carries a thermal array beside the camera — a warm body is unmistakable in 768 pixels, in the dark or in smoke, and thermal-plus-visible on one sweep is the strongest line-of-sight detector on the vehicle. A CO2 sensor rides the radar post for closed rooms, and an I2S microphone listens when the motors are stopped. The Pi runs MobileNet-SSD person detection on the camera at roughly 25 frames per second; fusing that with the thermal blob is the demo.

LD2410C is the primary presence sensor for anyone the camera cannot see: stationary or micro-moving people behind a door or a stud wall, plus breathing. LD2450 adds positions and speeds of moving targets and loses motionless bodies beyond about 2 m; it is not the primary detector for an immobile person. Neither outputs heartbeat measurements — and the 60 GHz breathing/heartbeat modules on the market were skipped: their heart-rate figure benchmarks at a 0.33 correlation against a chest strap, and the owned LD2410C already covers breathing. Start with one radar powered; test the two radar types separately, then together for interference. These sensors are not validated here for finding a motionless person behind concrete, rubble, fire or smoke. No detection must never be interpreted as proof that nobody is present.

Can four motors carry it?

Design mass 5.30 kg. Four load cases; the 5° carpet ramp at 0.3 m/s² governs. Lowest passing setting: DRV8825, heatsink + fan.

Margin = torque available ÷ torque required; acceptance ≥ 1.25 because the running torque is calculated, not measured. With a ~130 g power bank and your C920-class webcam the design sits at ~5.32 kg: 1.5 A misses the ramp by a hair (1.23), so the operating point is 1.7 A with the fan (1.40). Weigh the bank and the webcam; if either is heavier than budgeted, swap to the 50 g ELP camera or feed the Pi from an LM2596 instead. The one measurement that matters most: pull-out torque at 24 rpm, 1.7 A, on the real battery.

Power and connections

  • Pack -> XT60 (or the pack's own connector) -> two 22 AWG branches: shield VMOT (four DRV8825 -> four motors) and LM2596 IN for the servo rail. 22 AWG is rated 7 A against a 4 A burst here. No fuse, by your call — the connector is the disconnect, so keep the pack leads short and sleeved.
  • Power bank -> Pi USB-C. The bank must hold 5 V at 3 A: a 2.4 A bank browns out the moment the camera, thermal array and ESP32 are all drawing. Pi USB-A -> UNO R4 USB-C for logic power and serial. Fallback if the bank misbehaves: an LM2596 trimmed to 5.10 V on GPIO 2/4 and 6.
  • LM2596 OUT -> PCA9685 V+ terminal. Set to 6.0 V with a multimeter before connecting. Servo current returns through the PCA9685 terminal, never through a USB ground.
  • Pi -> camera by USB. Pi -> ESP32-S3 by USB-C; the ESP32 bridges both radar UARTs. Radars take 5 V power and 3.3 V UART signalling.
  • Four VL53L1X share I2C only after XSHUT (GPIO 5, 6, 13, 19) assigns unique addresses at boot. PCA9685 is on the same I2C bus at 0x40.
  • Single common ground at the shield GND terminal. Never put LiPo voltage on a logic rail.

Motors at 1.5 A per phase draw about 2 A from the pack at cruise — copper loss dominates, not motion. A 1200 mAh gel-blaster pack gives about 30 minutes of driving; the optional 5200 mAh pack about two hours. Holding current at rest costs the same as moving, so cut ENABLE (D8) when parked; mecanum wheels on a level floor do not roll away.

The owned three-pin switch is not in the load path until its DC rating is read off the body and is at least 10 A.

Motor and frame fit

Frame: two side rails along X with outer faces at Y = ±195; two cross rails along Y butted between them with outer faces at X = ±175. Rails sit at Z 71–91. Eight corner brackets, one top and one bottom at each joint, M5 T-nuts, 3.0 N·m maximum or the slot deforms.

Drive: each steel L-bracket goes inverted under a side rail near its end, foot against the rail underside on two M4 T-nuts, leg hanging down flush with the rail's outer face. The motor bolts to the inboard side of the leg with four M3×6 at 0.8 N·m (aluminium threads — the full 1.1 N·m an M3 can take will strip the flange). Shaft through the leg, coupler on the flat with blue threadlocker, wheel on the coupler. Axle at X = ±125, Z = 40. Motor body bottom at Z = 18.9 mm: that is your ground clearance.

Mecanum handedness: front-left and rear-right take the left-hand wheels, front-right and rear-left the right-hand. Check from above — the rollers on top of each wheel should point toward the chassis centre, forming an X.

Deck: two printed tiles 170 × 350 × 5 on the rail tops, six M5 T-nuts each, with 10 mm bosses printed in for every board — no bought standoffs. Boards sit on the bosses and are held by cable ties through printed slots (the Pi and PCA9685 take M2 screws from the M2 kit, since their holes are 2.7 mm). Battery cradle hangs under the deck centre on four M4×16 bolts with a printed retaining bar and two cable ties instead of a strap — battery bottom at Z 64, well above the motors. R4/shield stack rear-left, Pi rear-right, both bucks ahead of the R4, PCA9685 beside the Pi, ESP32 rear-centre ahead of the post. Turret base at X = +110 on the front tile with the thermal array in a printed saddle on top of the camera. Radar post on the rear cross rail through the deck, radars at Z = 300 facing forward, 100 mm apart, microphone between them, CO2 sensor on the post's rear face.

Compute before claiming it works

Load cases at the design mass, 80 mm wheels, mecanum roller factor √2, mechanical efficiency 0.85, carpet rolling coefficient 0.12: level cruise, level at 0.3 m/s², strafing, and a 5° carpet ramp at 0.3 m/s². The ramp governs. Available torque is holding torque × (I / 1.7 A) × 0.70, where 0.70 is a conservative low-speed pull-out factor that has not been measured. Full arithmetic, every input tagged measured / allocated / assumed, is in drivetrain.txt; every placement is in assembly.csv.

At 1/8 microstepping, 0.1 m/s is about 637 pulses/s per wheel and 0.3 m/s about 1,910. Custom four-axis firmware on the UNO R4 with a timer interrupt, acceleration limits, shared enable and a communication timeout; GRBL is unsuitable and would not run on the R4 anyway. Motor order at the shield: X = FL, Y = FR, Z = RL, A = RR with the D12/D13 jumpers fitted.

Set DRV8825 current with Vref = 5 × Rsense × I. With 0.100 Ω sense resistors, 1.5 A is 0.75 V; read the actual resistor first. Start at 1.5 A with heatsinks and the fan; 1.7 A is the reserve, not the default.

Before calling it working: caliper every part marked VERIFY before drilling the deck; measure pull-out torque at 24 rpm and 1.5 A on the real battery with a lever arm and the hanging scale; then all four wheel directions raised; loaded forward, reverse, strafe, turn; watchdog stop; temperature after 10 minutes; radar trials one at a time then together; turret tracking with the camera fitted; marked-route obstacle stops. None of these tests has been performed.

What I did NOT analyze

Fatigue, buckling, impact, vibration, roller wear, uneven floors and wheel unloading, combined-slip mecanum behaviour, motor and driver thermal equilibrium, battery sag under stall, firmware timing, fastener pull-out in PETG, deck deflection under the turret, servo torque with the camera fitted, motor-bearing radial rating (assumed 28 N — thinnest margin in the design), and radar detection at 300 mm height (Walleye measured floor-level radar as blind; this is a compromise to test).

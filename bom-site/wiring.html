Hound — wiring

Seven harness diagrams generated from one net list, rendered with WireViz (open source, Python + Graphviz), and cross-checked by script against the electrical model wiring.py: every load is fed from the rail the model says, every signal link has a wire, the Pi and R4 pins match. The model itself: 0 FAIL · 9 WARN · 5 VERIFY · 12 PROCEDURE · 59 PASS; pack 2.2 A avg / 5.29 A peak, bank 1.49 A avg / 2.44 A peak, Pi USB 1.0 A of 1.2.

Can the PCA9685 be wired straight to the CNC Shield's pins? Yes. The shield breaks out the R4's A4 (SDA) and A5 (SCL) — the R4's primary Wire bus, 5 V logic — plus 5V and GND pins. That is diagram 3B. But the rover is built the other way (3A): the Raspberry Pi drives the PCA9685 over its I2C-1 (BCM 2/3) at 3.3 V, and turret.py / station.py already work that way. Keep 3A. Switch to 3B only if the MG996R jitters or ignores 3.3 V PWM — then the shield's 5 V logic fixes it, and the R4 firmware needs a tilt command (not written yet). Never wire both: a PCA9685 with 5 V pull-ups must not touch the Pi's SDA/SCL.
The power scheme, simplified (v3.1). No lever nuts, no resistor boards, one buck. Pack → Deans pull-plug on the deck (that plug is the e-stop) → the CNC shield's VMOT/GND screw terminal with the 1000 µF cap. The same two screws take a second, thinner wire each to the XL4015 (set to 5.0 V), whose output goes into the PCA9685's V+/GND terminal. The tilt servo sits on channel 0; the fan plugs onto channel 15's V+ and GND pins, because every channel header carries the rail. The Pi runs on its 3 A bank and powers the R4, the hub, the webcam and, through the CP2102 adapters' 5V pins, the three radars (four wires each). The ESP32-S3 CSI node has its own small bank. Thermal, ToF ×2, mic and the PCA9685 logic hang on the Pi's 3.3 V header. No pack voltage divider and no alarm: 30-minute timer, then meter the pack.

ESP32 #3 runs csi_rx_udp ON the rover from its OWN small power bank on its USB-C — nothing else plugs into it, no wire touches the rover's electronics. It streams the CSI of every probe to the Pi hotspot (channel 6), UDP port 5005. ESP32 #1 is the spare.
  • driver-orient — DRV8825 into the shield socket with its EN pin over the socket's EN label (the trimpot ends up toward the shield's power terminal on genuine Pololu layouts; clones vary — go by the label). Backwards = dead driver on power-up, no warning
  • fan — The 40 mm 5 V fan plugs onto the PCA9685's channel-15 header: red → the middle (V+) pin, black → the bottom (GND) pin. The XL4015 is set to 5.0 V for exactly this; the tilt servo is fine at 5 V.
  • ground — Servo/fan/ESP32 return: PCA9685 V+ GND terminal → XL4015 OUT− (its own wire). Radars return through their adapters (USB side). Pack current never flows through a board GND pin or a USB cable.
  • i2c-chain — The Pi header has only two 3V3 pins and one SDA/SCL pair, but five boards want 3V3 and three want SDA/SCL — one Dupont female per pin. Chain through the boards' second header/STEMMA port (Adafruit-pattern MLX/ToF/PCA boards have one), or use a mini breadboard as the 3V3/SDA/SCL splitter. Never force two females onto one pin.
  • microstep — AS BUILT (v1.0 best drive): DRV8825 1/32 = M0 + M2 → caps on MS1 and MS3 under each driver, MS2 open (eight caps), plus the D12 and D13 jumpers on the A header = ten jumpers total. Caps go on before the drivers. 1/16 (one cap, MS3 only, firmware m 16) is the faster alternative — a drive-setting change, ask first.
  • pack-splice — The shield's VMOT/GND screw terminal is the only pack splice: the 14 AWG pack lead and the 22 AWG XL4015 input wire share each screw (2.4 mm² total, inside the 5.08 mm terminal's range). Strip 7 mm, twist the two conductors together before inserting, tug-test both.
  • phases — Motor coil pairs by multimeter: the two leads that show ~2 Ω are one coil. A coil split across the 2B/2A–1A/1B header halves makes a motor buzz and not turn.
  • radar-power — Each radar is a 4-wire job to its own CP2102: VCC → the adapter's 5V pin, GND → GND, TX → RXD, RX → TXD. The three radars add 260 mA to the Pi's USB budget (checked above). The adapter must be a 3.3 V-logic type: meter its TXD pin at idle — 3.3 V, not 5 V
  • sequence — Power-up: bank → Pi → R4 (USB) FIRST, then the pack at the T-plug. Power-down and BEFORE every flash/reset of the R4: pull the pack plug first. Reason: DRV8825 nENABLE has a pull-down, so while the R4 is dead or resetting all four drivers sit at full holding current (no pull-up resistor fitted — by choice; the plug order replaces it).
  • vref — Read the sense resistor on one driver: R100 → Vref = 0.75 V for 1.5 A; R200 → 1.50 V. Set all four with the multimeter, motors unplugged, before any motor is connected.

Verify before power (VERIFY / WARN)

  • V-chain — webcam sees 4.53 V at its 500 mA descriptor (4.57 V at a typical 250 mA) through its own captive 1.5 m cable; USB floor 4.75 V, most cameras run to ~4.4 V. If it drops out at 1080p, run 720p — its cable cannot be shortened
  • V-chain — A 1 m 28 AWG phone cable to the Pi would drop 1.04 V → 3.86 V at the Pi = brownout. The bank cable is a spec: ≤ 0.2 m with 22 AWG or heavier power conductors (a short '3 A fast-charge' cable)
  • connector — Motor phases run 1.5 A through Dupont 2.54 contacts rated 2.0 A — inside rating but the crimp quality is the margin. A contact that heats in the wheels-up test gets re-crimped or soldered.
  • connector — Tilt servo stall 2.3 A through the PCA9685's 2.54 mm channel header (rated 2.0 A). Momentary, and how every servo board works; keep stalls short with tilt limits in software
  • enable — No EN pull-up fitted: during an R4 reset (~1 s at boot, every sketch upload) all four drivers are ENABLED at full holding current. Harmless for a second; NOT harmless if you flash repeatedly with the pack plugged in (drivers heat). So: pack unplugged whenever the USB cable is doing anything but driving.
  • protection — No fuse, no cell alarm, no voltage divider (all declined for simplicity): nothing stops the pack from over-discharging. Rule: a full pack (12.6 V) gives ~45 min of driving — set a 30-minute timer, then meter the pack; stop at 11.1 V, never below 10.5 V. A pack that reads 6–7 V is dead (pack A).
  • usb — Pi 5 only: default 600 mA USB cap → set usb_max_current_enable=1, or use the Pi 4 this file assumes.
  • wire — bank → Pi USB-C cable (SPEC: ≤ 0.2 m, 22 AWG or heavier power conductors): 22 AWG × 0.2 m, peak 2.44 A (ampacity 7 A, USB-C 3.0 A), round-trip drop 52 mV = 1.0%
  • wire — Pi USB-A → webcam (captive cable, 1.5 m): 28 AWG × 1.5 m, peak 0.30 A (ampacity 1.4 A, USB-A 1.5 A), round-trip drop 192 mV = 3.8%
  • bank — Load-test the bank at 2.5 A for 60 s with the meter on the Pi's 5 V pin: below 4.8 V the bank is not a 3 A bank.
  • bank-cable — If the bank's output is USB-C and the Pi 4 is an early board (pre-2020, rev 1.1), an e-marked C-to-C cable will not power it — use an A-to-C cable or a plain C-to-C. Later Pi 4s and all Pi 5s are fine
  • i2c — Before connecting SDA/SCL: power each I2C module alone and meter SDA to GND — it must read ≤ 3.3 V. A module whose pull-ups go to 5 V goes on 3.3 V power or not on the Pi.
  • i2c3 — BCM 4/5 have NO on-board I2C pull-ups (BCM 2/3 have 1.8 kΩ). Adafruit-pattern MLX90640 breakouts carry 10 kΩ pull-ups, so no resistors are needed — check with i2cdetect -y 3 (expect 0x33); if it is absent, that module has none and needs 2.2–4.7 kΩ to 3.3 V. And w1-gpio (1-Wire) defaults to BCM 4 — leave that overlay off

If the servo jitters or ignores commands, that is the level: diagram 3B (shield A4/A5, 5 V logic) fixes it. Do NOT raise VCC to 5 V while SDA/SCL touch the Pi

=== PROCEDURES AND WARNINGS (print these on the sheet; they come from the electrical model wiring.py) ===
  [WARN] webcam sees 4.53 V at its 500 mA descriptor (4.57 V at a typical 250 mA) through its own captive 1.5 m cable; USB floor 4.75 V, most cameras run to ~4.4 V. If it drops out at 1080p, run 720p — its cable cannot be shortened
  [WARN] A 1 m 28 AWG phone cable to the Pi would drop 1.04 V → 3.86 V at the Pi = brownout. The bank cable is a spec: ≤ 0.2 m with 22 AWG or heavier power conductors (a short '3 A fast-charge' cable)
  [WARN] Motor phases run 1.5 A through Dupont 2.54 contacts rated 2.0 A — inside rating but the crimp quality is the margin. A contact that heats in the wheels-up test gets re-crimped or soldered.
  [WARN] Tilt servo stall 2.3 A through the PCA9685's 2.54 mm channel header (rated 2.0 A). Momentary, and how every servo board works; keep stalls short with tilt limits in software
  [WARN] No EN pull-up fitted: during an R4 reset (~1 s at boot, every sketch upload) all four drivers are ENABLED at full holding current. Harmless for a second; NOT harmless if you flash repeatedly with the pack plugged in (drivers heat). So: pack unplugged whenever the USB cable is doing anything but driving.
  [WARN] No fuse, no cell alarm, no voltage divider (all declined for simplicity): nothing stops the pack from over-discharging. Rule: a full pack (12.6 V) gives ~45 min of driving — set a 30-minute timer, then meter the pack; stop at 11.1 V, never below 10.5 V. A pack that reads 6–7 V is dead (pack A).
  [WARN] Pi 5 only: default 600 mA USB cap → set usb_max_current_enable=1, or use the Pi 4 this file assumes.
  [WARN] bank → Pi USB-C cable (SPEC: ≤ 0.2 m, 22 AWG or heavier power conductors): 22 AWG × 0.2 m, peak 2.44 A (ampacity 7 A, USB-C 3.0 A), round-trip drop 52 mV = 1.0%
  [WARN] Pi USB-A → webcam (captive cable, 1.5 m): 28 AWG × 1.5 m, peak 0.30 A (ampacity 1.4 A, USB-A 1.5 A), round-trip drop 192 mV = 3.8%
  [VERIFY] Load-test the bank at 2.5 A for 60 s with the meter on the Pi's 5 V pin: below 4.8 V the bank is not a 3 A bank.
  [VERIFY] If the bank's output is USB-C and the Pi 4 is an early board (pre-2020, rev 1.1), an e-marked C-to-C cable will not power it — use an A-to-C cable or a plain C-to-C. Later Pi 4s and all Pi 5s are fine
  [VERIFY] Before connecting SDA/SCL: power each I2C module alone and meter SDA to GND — it must read ≤ 3.3 V. A module whose pull-ups go to 5 V goes on 3.3 V power or not on the Pi.
  [VERIFY] BCM 4/5 have NO on-board I2C pull-ups (BCM 2/3 have 1.8 kΩ). Adafruit-pattern MLX90640 breakouts carry 10 kΩ pull-ups, so no resistors are needed — check with i2cdetect -y 3 (expect 0x33); if it is absent, that module has none and needs 2.2–4.7 kΩ to 3.3 V. And w1-gpio (1-Wire) defaults to BCM 4 — leave that overlay off
  [VERIFY] PCA9685 VCC is 3.3 V, so the tilt servo gets 3.3 V PWM — the signal line only carries a logic level, and MG996R-class servos accept it (confirmed in use with Pi + PCA9685 builds). If the servo jitters or ignores commands, that is the level: diagram 3B (shield A4/A5, 5 V logic) fixes it. Do NOT raise VCC to 5 V while SDA/SCL touch the Pi
  [PROCEDURE] 1000 µF electrolytic across VMOT: stripe = negative, to the GND terminal. Reversed, it vents on the first power-up
  [PROCEDURE] CSI v2: ESP32 #2 runs csi_tx OFF the rover (dropped as far along the wall as the room allows, any USB 5 V, channel 6, 100 Hz probes). ESP32 #3 runs csi_rx_udp ON the rover from its OWN small power bank on its USB-C — nothing else plugs into it, no wire touches the rover's electronics. It streams the CSI of every probe to the Pi hotspot (channel 6), UDP port 5005. ESP32 #1 is the spare.
  [PROCEDURE] DRV8825 into the shield socket with its EN pin over the socket's EN label (the trimpot ends up toward the shield's power terminal on genuine Pololu layouts; clones vary — go by the label). Backwards = dead driver on power-up, no warning
  [PROCEDURE] The 40 mm 5 V fan plugs onto the PCA9685's channel-15 header: red → the middle (V+) pin, black → the bottom (GND) pin. The XL4015 is set to 5.0 V for exactly this; the tilt servo is fine at 5 V.
  [PROCEDURE] Servo/fan/ESP32 return: PCA9685 V+ GND terminal → XL4015 OUT− (its own wire). Radars return through their adapters (USB side). Pack current never flows through a board GND pin or a USB cable.
  [PROCEDURE] The Pi header has only two 3V3 pins and one SDA/SCL pair, but five boards want 3V3 and three want SDA/SCL — one Dupont female per pin. Chain through the boards' second header/STEMMA port (Adafruit-pattern MLX/ToF/PCA boards have one), or use a mini breadboard as the 3V3/SDA/SCL splitter. Never force two females onto one pin.
  [PROCEDURE] AS BUILT (v1.0 best drive): DRV8825 1/32 = M0 + M2 → caps on MS1 and MS3 under each driver, MS2 open (eight caps), plus the D12 and D13 jumpers on the A header = ten jumpers total. Caps go on before the drivers. 1/16 (one cap, MS3 only, firmware m 16) is the faster alternative — a drive-setting change, ask first.
  [PROCEDURE] The shield's VMOT/GND screw terminal is the only pack splice: the 14 AWG pack lead and the 22 AWG XL4015 input wire share each screw (2.4 mm² total, inside the 5.08 mm terminal's range). Strip 7 mm, twist the two conductors together before inserting, tug-test both.
  [PROCEDURE] Motor coil pairs by multimeter: the two leads that show ~2 Ω are one coil. A coil split across the 2B/2A–1A/1B header halves makes a motor buzz and not turn.
  [PROCEDURE] Each radar is a 4-wire job to its own CP2102: VCC → the adapter's 5V pin, GND → GND, TX → RXD, RX → TXD. The three radars add 260 mA to the Pi's USB budget (checked above). The adapter must be a 3.3 V-logic type: meter its TXD pin at idle — 3.3 V, not 5 V
  [PROCEDURE] Power-up: bank → Pi → R4 (USB) FIRST, then the pack at the T-plug. Power-down and BEFORE every flash/reset of the R4: pull the pack plug first. Reason: DRV8825 nENABLE has a pull-down, so while the R4 is dead or resetting all four drivers sit at full holding current (no pull-up resistor fitted — by choice; the plug order replaces it).
  [PROCEDURE] Read the sense resistor on one driver: R100 → Vref = 0.75 V for 1.5 A; R200 → 1.50 V. Set all four with the multimeter, motors unplugged, before any motor is connected.

2. Each LD2410C and the LD2450 has exactly TWO GND wires: one to WAGO5_N, one to its CP2102 GND. Their VCC/5V comes from WAGO5_P, never from the CP2102's 5V pin.
3. Nothing on the Pi 40-pin header carries 5 V: all I2C/I2S devices are on 3V3 pins; PCA9685 VCC = 3V3 in 3A.
4. The MLX90640 is on BCM 4/5 (i2c3), alone. The two VL53L1X and the PCA9685 are on BCM 2/3 (I2C-1). Addresses on I2C-1: 0x40, 0x29, 0x30 — no clash.
5. Motor bus: WAGO_P → SHIELD_PWR VMOT and WAGO_N → SHIELD_PWR GND, 22 AWG, with the 1000 µF cap noted, stripe to GND.
6. XL4015 OUT− goes to the PCA9685 GND TERMINAL by its own wire (servo return); it is not shared with any signal ground.
7. The E-STOP sits in the pack + lead between the T-plug and WAGO_P; the Pi is on the USB bank, not on the pack.
8. The divider is 47 kΩ WAGO_P → A3, 10 kΩ A3 → WAGO_N; A3 is the shield's CoolEn pin.
9. ESP32-S3 no.3 is fed at its 5V pin from WAGO5_P and its USB port is marked "never while 5V is fed".
10. The shield's four motor headers map X=BL, Y=FL, Z=BR, A=FR; each motor's coil pairs are found with a meter, colours are indicative only.
11. Power-up order is printed: bank → Pi → R4 (USB) → then the pack.

Hound — wiring

Seven harness diagrams generated from one net list, rendered with WireViz (open source, Python + Graphviz), and cross-checked by script against the electrical model wiring.py: every load is fed from the rail the model says, every signal link has a wire, the Pi and R4 pins match. The model itself: 0 FAIL · 7 WARN · 5 VERIFY · 12 PROCEDURE · 64 PASS; pack 2.5 A avg / 6.3 A peak, bank 1.23 A avg / 2.24 A peak, Pi USB 0.8 A of 1.2.

Can the PCA9685 be wired straight to the CNC Shield's pins? Yes. The shield breaks out the R4's A4 (SDA) and A5 (SCL) — the R4's primary Wire bus, 5 V logic — plus 5V and GND pins. That is diagram 3B. But the rover is built the other way (3A): the Raspberry Pi drives the PCA9685 over its I2C-1 (BCM 2/3) at 3.3 V, and turret.py / station.py already work that way. Keep 3A. Switch to 3B only if the MG996R jitters or ignores 3.3 V PWM — then the shield's 5 V logic fixes it, and the R4 firmware needs a tilt command (not written yet). Never wire both: a PCA9685 with 5 V pull-ups must not touch the Pi's SDA/SCL.
The sensors. The two LD2410C radars each go through their own CP2102 adapter into the Pi's USB hub: only TX, RX and GND on the adapter; power from the 5 V Wagos. The LD2450 (the long one) is the third radar: it tracks up to three moving targets with x/y position and is what turns "someone is 2.1 m away" into "someone is 2.1 m away, 0.4 m to the left". It needs a third CP2102 (on order or skipped) and until then stays unplugged. Thermal array (MLX90640) rides on the tilt bracket beside the webcam and hangs on the Pi's second I2C bus alone, so it can run fast. ToF ×2, mic, PCA9685 on the Pi's first I2C/I2S. Pack side: e-stop → two Wagos → shield, XL4015 (6.0 V servo), LM2596 (5.0 V fan + radars + ESP32 no.3). Pi on its own USB bank.

Build procedures (from wiring.py)

  • cap-polarity — 1000 µF electrolytic across VMOT: stripe = negative, to the GND terminal. Reversed, it vents on the first power-up
  • csi-nodes — CSI v2: ESP32 #2 runs csi_tx OFF the rover (dropped as far along the wall as the room allows, any USB 5 V, channel 6, 100 Hz probes). ESP32 #3 runs csi_rx_udp ON the rover from the LM2596 5 V pin — USB never connected while the 5 V pin is fed — and streams the CSI of every probe to the Pi hotspot, which must be on channel 6, UDP port 5005. ESP32 #1 is the spare.
  • driver-orient — DRV8825 into the shield socket with its EN pin over the socket's EN label (the trimpot ends up toward the shield's power terminal on genuine Pololu layouts; clones vary — go by the label). Backwards = dead driver on power-up, no warning
  • fan-rail — The 40 mm fan must be a 5 V fan for the LM2596 rail. A 12 V fan goes on the pack side (Wago +/−, 11.1 V nominal) instead — check the label before it is wired
  • ground — Servo return: PCA9685 V+ GND terminal → XL4015 OUT− (its own wire). Motor return: shield GND terminal → Wago. Neither ever through a board GND pin or a USB cable.
  • lm-dist — LM2596 OUT feeds five loads (fan, two LD2410C, LD2450, ESP32 #3): six conductors per polarity. Fan straight off the LM2596 OUT terminals, the four sensors from a 5-way Wago pair. Never daisy-chain through a load's connector
  • microstep — AS BUILT (v1.0 best drive): DRV8825 1/32 = M0 + M2 → caps on MS1 and MS3 under each driver, MS2 open (eight caps), plus the D12 and D13 jumpers on the A header = ten jumpers total. Caps go on before the drivers. 1/16 (one cap, MS3 only, firmware m 16) is the faster alternative — a drive-setting change, ask first.
  • phases — Motor coil pairs by multimeter: the two leads that show ~2 Ω are one coil. A coil split across the 2B/2A–1A/1B header halves makes a motor buzz and not turn.
  • radar-gnd — Each radar's GND gets TWO wires: one to the LM2596 OUT− Wago (its power return, 260 mA for the three) and one to its CP2102's GND pin (signal reference). Never power a radar from the CP2102's 5 V pin: that is the Pi USB budget, and the return would run through the Pi
  • sequence — Power-up: bank → Pi → R4 (USB) FIRST, then the pack at the T-plug. DRV8825 nENABLE floats LOW = enabled; with VMOT alone four motors sit at full holding current with nothing commanding them.
  • vbat — Pack-voltage sense: 47 kΩ from the Wago + to the shield CoolEn pin (A3), 10 kΩ from A3 to the shield GND pin. 12.6 V → 2.21 V at A3 (R4 ADC ref 5 V). Then enable the cutoff with V 10.5 over serial; until the divider exists vcut stays 0.
  • vref — Read the sense resistor on one driver: R100 → Vref = 0.75 V for 1.5 A; R200 → 1.50 V. Set all four with the multimeter, motors unplugged, before any motor is connected.

Verify before power (VERIFY / WARN)

  • V-chain — webcam sees 4.42 V at its 500 mA descriptor (4.58 V at a typical 250 mA) through its own captive 1.5 m cable; USB floor 4.75 V, most cameras run to ~4.4 V. If it drops out at 1080p, run 720p — its cable cannot be shortened
  • V-chain — A 1 m 28 AWG phone cable to the Pi would drop 0.95 V → 3.95 V at the Pi = brownout. The bank cable is a spec: ≤ 0.3 m with ≥ 24 AWG power conductors (any '3 A fast-charge' cable)
  • connector — Motor phases run 1.5 A through Dupont 2.54 contacts rated 2.0 A — inside rating but the crimp quality is the margin. A contact that heats in the wheels-up test gets re-crimped or soldered.
  • connector — Tilt servo stall 2.5 A through the PCA9685's 2.54 mm channel header (rated 2.0 A). Momentary, and how every servo board works; keep stalls short with tilt limits in software
  • enable — DRV8825 nENABLE has an internal pull-down: during the R4's ~1 s reset window (boot, watchdog, sketch upload) D8 floats and all four drivers are ENABLED at full holding current. Add a 10 kΩ pull-up from the shield's EN pin to its 5 V header (consumables). The R4-unpowered case is still the power-up order
  • protection — No fuse on the pack and no cell alarm (declined). A 2200 mAh 80C pack delivers >70 A into a short; the T-plug is a disconnect, not protection. Listed under Safety as reviewer-recommended.
  • usb — Pi 5 only: default 600 mA USB cap → set usb_max_current_enable=1, or use the Pi 4 this file assumes.
  • bank — Load-test the bank at 2.5 A for 60 s with the meter on the Pi's 5 V pin: below 4.8 V the bank is not a 3 A bank.
  • bank-cable — If the bank's output is USB-C and the Pi 4 is an early board (pre-2020, rev 1.1), an e-marked C-to-C cable will not power it — use an A-to-C cable or a plain C-to-C. Later Pi 4s and all Pi 5s are fine
  • i2c — Before connecting SDA/SCL: power each I2C module alone and meter SDA to GND — it must read ≤ 3.3 V. A module whose pull-ups go to 5 V goes on 3.3 V power or not on the Pi.
  • i2c3 — BCM 4/5 have NO on-board I2C pull-ups (BCM 2/3 have 1.8 kΩ). The MLX90640 module must carry its own, or add 4.7 kΩ from SDA and SCL to 3.3 V (consumables). And w1-gpio (1-Wire) defaults to BCM 4 — leave that overlay off
  • servo-pwm — PCA9685 VCC is 3.3 V, so the tilt servo gets 3.3 V PWM. MG996R clones normally accept it; if the servo jitters or ignores commands, that is the level. Do NOT raise VCC to 5 V — the board's I2C pull-ups follow VCC and would put 5 V on the Pi's SDA/SCL. A 74HCT125 buffer fixes it

  [WARN] Motor phases run 1.5 A through Dupont 2.54 contacts rated 2.0 A — inside rating but the crimp quality is the margin. A contact that heats in the wheels-up test gets re-crimped or soldered.
  [WARN] Tilt servo stall 2.5 A through the PCA9685's 2.54 mm channel header (rated 2.0 A). Momentary, and how every servo board works; keep stalls short with tilt limits in software
  [WARN] DRV8825 nENABLE has an internal pull-down: during the R4's ~1 s reset window (boot, watchdog, sketch upload) D8 floats and all four drivers are ENABLED at full holding current.   [WARN] No fuse on the pack and no cell alarm (declined). A 2200 mAh 80C pack delivers >70 A into a short; the T-plug is a disconnect, not protection. Listed under Safety as reviewer-recommended.
  [WARN] Pi 5 only: default 600 mA USB cap → set usb_max_current_enable=1, or use the Pi 4 this file assumes.
  [VERIFY] Load-test the bank at 2.5 A for 60 s with the meter on the Pi's 5 V pin: below 4.8 V the bank is not a 3 A bank.
  [VERIFY] If the bank's output is USB-C and the Pi 4 is an early board (pre-2020, rev 1.1), an e-marked C-to-C cable will not power it — use an A-to-C cable or a plain C-to-C. Later Pi 4s and all Pi 5s are fine
  [VERIFY] Before connecting SDA/SCL: power each I2C module alone and meter SDA to GND — it must read ≤ 3.3 V. A module whose pull-ups go to 5 V goes on 3.3 V power or not on the Pi.
  [VERIFY] BCM 4/5 have NO on-board I2C pull-ups (BCM 2/3 have 1.8 kΩ). The MLX90640 module must carry its own, or add 4.7 kΩ from SDA and SCL to 3.3 V (consumables). And w1-gpio (1-Wire) defaults to BCM 4 — leave that overlay off
  [VERIFY] PCA9685 VCC is 3.3 V, so the tilt servo gets 3.3 V PWM. MG996R clones normally accept it; if the servo jitters or ignores commands, that is the level. Do NOT raise VCC to 5 V — the board's I2C pull-ups follow VCC and would put 5 V on the Pi's SDA/SCL. A 74HCT125 buffer fixes it
  [PROCEDURE] 1000 µF electrolytic across VMOT: stripe = negative, to the GND terminal. Reversed, it vents on the first power-up
  [PROCEDURE] CSI v2: ESP32 #2 runs csi_tx OFF the rover (dropped as far along the wall as the room allows, any USB 5 V, channel 6, 100 Hz probes).   [PROCEDURE] The 40 mm fan must be a 5 V fan for the LM2596 rail. A 12 V fan goes on the pack side (Wago +/−, 11.1 V nominal) instead — check the label before it is wired
  [PROCEDURE] Servo return: PCA9685 V+ GND terminal → XL4015 OUT− (its own wire). Motor return: shield GND terminal → Wago. Neither ever through a board GND pin or a USB cable.
